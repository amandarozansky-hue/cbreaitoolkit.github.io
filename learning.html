Module 1

AI fundamentals

Understand what you're actually working with — in plain language, no technical background required.

Generative AI is software that creates new content — text, images, or code — by learning patterns from vast amounts of existing content. When you type into Microsoft Copilot or Claude, you're interacting with a Large Language Model (LLM).

An LLM has been trained on hundreds of billions of words from books, websites, and research papers. It learned the statistical patterns of language: which words follow which, how ideas connect, what a professional email sounds like versus a casual message.

It does not "think" or "know" things the way a human does. It predicts the most useful next word given everything you've written. Done billions of times, this produces fluent, coherent text — often impressively useful.

💡
Key implication: Output quality depends almost entirely on input quality. A vague prompt produces a generic result. A specific, well-structured prompt produces something you can actually use.

CBRE has three main AI tools available. Each has a different strength — knowing which to reach for saves significant time.

Microsoft Copilot
Microsoft 365
Integrated into Outlook, Word, Excel, Teams. Best for drafting emails, summarising documents, creating slide outlines, and analysing spreadsheets within your existing workflow.
Ellis AI
CBRE Platform
CBRE's internal AI with access to proprietary data and market databases. Best for market analysis, comparable transactions, and research tasks that need CBRE-specific information.
Claude
General AI
Excellent for long-form analysis, complex reasoning, and structured documents. Works best with detailed, well-structured prompts and produces especially nuanced output.
📌
Rule of thumb: Use Copilot for speed within M365 apps. Use Ellis for anything needing CBRE data or market comparables. Use Claude for complex analysis or long documents.

AI tools are powerful, but they have real limitations you must account for before using any output professionally.

  • Hallucinations: AI can confidently state incorrect facts — wrong statistics, fabricated sources, outdated figures. Always verify any specific data point before using it professionally.
  • Knowledge cutoffs: Most models have a training cutoff date and don't know about recent transactions, market movements, or current events. Never rely on AI for live market data.
  • No memory between sessions: Unless you're in a continued conversation, the AI starts fresh each time. It doesn't remember what you discussed yesterday.
  • Context limits: AI tools can only process a certain amount of text at once. Very long documents may be truncated or partially misinterpreted.
  • Plausible ≠ accurate: AI is trained to produce text that sounds correct. Fluency and factual accuracy are entirely independent — a beautifully written paragraph can contain serious errors.
✅
The rule: AI output is a first draft, not a final product. Your professional judgement, industry knowledge, and fact-checking are always the last line of defence before anything goes to a client.

What you type into an AI tool may be stored, logged, or used for model improvement depending on the tool and your organisation's enterprise settings. Apply this simple rule:

Never put into an AI tool anything you wouldn't be comfortable seeing on the front page of a newspaper.

  • Do not input: client names or identifying details, deal values or transaction specifics, confidential financial data, personal data of individuals, proprietary CBRE strategy or pricing
  • Safe to input: anonymised summaries, publicly listed property descriptions, your own draft text for review, general market questions, structural and formatting tasks
  • Ellis AI is the safest tool for CBRE-specific data as it operates within CBRE's controlled environment
⚠️
GDPR reminder: Inputting personal data about clients, colleagues, or counterparties into external AI tools may constitute a data breach under GDPR. When in doubt, anonymise first — replace names with [Client A] and values with [£amount].
Module 2

Prompt engineering

The craft of giving AI clear enough instructions that it produces something useful on the first attempt.

The Framework

The 4-element RCTF structure

R
Role
Tell the AI who it is and what expertise to draw on. This shapes vocabulary, depth, and perspective.
"You are a senior commercial real estate advisor with 15 years' experience in logistics markets…"
C
Context
Provide the background the AI needs. More relevant detail means less generic output.
"I have just completed a site visit with a 500-person fintech firm relocating from Canary Wharf…"
T
Task
State precisely what you want. One clear, specific task per prompt produces better results.
"Write a follow-up email addressing their concern and proposing a second viewing with the landlord…"
F
Format
Specify length, structure, tone, and constraints. Without this, AI defaults to a format that may not fit.
"Under 150 words. Professional but warm. Subject line included. End with a clear call-to-action."

Example

See the difference RCTF makes

Without RCTF
Write an email about the property visit.
Vague — AI has to guess audience, tone, purpose, and length
With RCTF
You are a senior leasing broker [ROLE]. I visited a 12,000 sqft Grade A office in EC2 yesterday with a fintech occupier who expressed strong interest but had concerns about the breakout space [CONTEXT]. Write a follow-up email addressing their concern, referencing the building's upcoming fit-out allowance, and proposing a second viewing with the landlord present [TASK]. Keep it under 150 words, warm-professional tone, clear subject line, end with a specific call-to-action [FORMAT].
Specific — AI produces a usable draft on the first attempt

Common pitfalls

5 mistakes that produce weak output

1
Being too vague
"Write something about this deal" gives the AI nothing. It produces something that sounds right but fits nothing specific.
Name the deal, client, and goal
2
No format specified
Without instructions, AI outputs a wall of text. If you needed bullet points, a table, or 100 words — say so explicitly.
Always include length, structure, tone
3
Forgetting the audience
AI doesn't know if your output is for a FTSE CEO or a first-time buyer. It defaults to a middle-ground that fits no one perfectly.
Specify who will read it and why
4
Accepting the first output
The first response is rarely the best one. AI improves dramatically when you iterate — refine and redirect in the same conversation.
Always ask for at least one revision
5
Pasting sensitive data
Client names, transaction values, and personal data should never go into external AI tools. Anonymise before you paste.
Use [Client A], [£amount], [Property]

Technique

How to iterate your way to a better result

01
Run your first prompt
Use the RCTF framework. Don't aim for perfection — aim for a useful starting point. Even a rough output is faster to refine than writing from scratch.
02
Identify what's off
Is the tone wrong? Too long? Missing a key point? Focus on the single most important issue first. Changing everything at once makes it hard to know what worked.
03
Give a targeted follow-up
"Make it more formal." "Cut to 100 words." "Add a paragraph on the exit strategy." Stay in the same conversation — context carries over.
04
Add your expertise
Paste the best version into your document and layer in your on-the-ground knowledge, client relationships, and professional judgement. That final 20% is yours.

Writing a Prompt from Scratch
When the library doesn't have exactly what you need, build your own using the RCTF framework.
  1. 1Write the Role: who should the AI be, and what expertise should it draw on?
  2. 2Write the Context: what's the specific situation? Include property details, client background, relevant data
  3. 3Write the Task: one clear, specific instruction. One task per prompt produces better results
  4. 4Add Format: word count, structure (bullets/prose/table), tone, audience. Run, assess, iterate.

Reviewing & Improving AI Output
The professional discipline of turning a good AI draft into something genuinely excellent and client-ready.
  1. 1Read the entire output before editing. Is the structure right? Is the overall tone right?
  2. 2Verify every specific fact: figures, names, dates, market data. Change or remove anything unconfirmed.
  3. 3Add your professional insight — the on-the-ground knowledge AI doesn't have. This is what makes it uniquely yours.
  4. 4Read it aloud. If you wouldn't say it, don't send it. Adjust until it sounds like you.

Module 4

AI glossary

The terms you'll encounter in AI tools, conversations, and documentation — defined without jargon.

Core
Generative AI
AI that creates new content (text, images, code) rather than just classifying or predicting from existing data.
Core
Large Language Model (LLM)
The technology behind Copilot, Claude, and ChatGPT. Trained on vast amounts of text to predict and generate language.
Core
Prompt
The instruction or question you give an AI tool. The quality of your prompt is the primary driver of output quality.
Know This
Hallucination
When AI confidently states something incorrect. A structural risk of LLMs — not a bug. Always verify specific facts before using them professionally.
Know This
Knowledge Cutoff
The date after which an AI model has no training data. It genuinely doesn't know about events, deals, or market moves after this date.
Technical
Context Window
The maximum amount of text an AI can process at once — roughly its "working memory." Older content may be forgotten in very long sessions.
Technical
Token
The unit AI uses to measure text — roughly ¾ of a word. Models have limits on how many tokens they can process in one request.
Technical
RAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation. An architecture where AI searches a document database before generating a response — how Ellis AI accesses CBRE data.
Technique
Few-shot Prompting
Including 2–3 examples of your desired output inside the prompt. Dramatically improves consistency and style matching.
Technique
Chain-of-thought
Adding "Think step by step" to your prompt, which makes AI reason through a problem before concluding. Improves accuracy on complex tasks.
Technique
System Prompt
Background instructions given to an AI before a conversation starts. Shapes its persona, constraints, and default behaviour throughout the session.
Technical
Temperature
A setting controlling how creative vs. predictable AI responses are. Higher = more varied output; lower = more focused. Most professional tools set this automatically.
